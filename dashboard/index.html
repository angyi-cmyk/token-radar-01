How the ranking scores a protocol

Every row on the board is priced by one function fitted on the snapshot itself, and every term in it earned its place in a held-out test. The model fits log(market cap) = a + b·log(TVL) + category offset on the snapshot’s live-token comparables; the slope comes out near 0.46, sublinear, not 1.0. Growth, age and chain breadth were each tested the same way and dropped: adding any of them makes the held-out predictions worse. The term ledger on the board shows the numbers, including the old radar-2 formula’s leave-one-out R² of about −0.19 — worse than guessing the mean — which is why it was replaced.

  1. TVL is the anchor, fitted. The slope on log(TVL) is estimated from live tokens, so a protocol holding more value implies a larger token, but less than proportionally.
  2. Category offsets are shrunk. Each category’s median residual is pulled toward zero by n/(n+8), so a thin category cannot swing a row on one or two comparables.
  3. Launch odds come from a fitted hazard. A logistic fit of “has launched” on log(age) over the live-vs-pre-token peers, validated held-out against the raw age buckets, gives P(launch in 12m / 24m) for each pre-token protocol.
  4. Ranges are shown, not hidden. The 80% interval is the 80th percentile of the absolute leave-one-out residuals, so a wide range is a wide range.
  5. Farm verdicts are costed. Gas plus capital plus the farmer’s time at $25/hour, against P(12m) × implied value × 0.1% assumed airdrop share: FARM only when the expected value covers the cost. Every verdict shows its expected-value multiple, so the reader sees by how much each row clears the bar.
  6. Nothing is claimed that the tests do not support. The cover states the four-test verdict in one line; the radar-6 round hunted for a stronger value signal — interactions, chain proxies, 90-day TVL momentum — and kept none of them, because each failed a blind test or cannot be computed from this snapshot.

Implied value is a model, not a price and not a promise. A protocol on this board may never issue a token, and a token that does launch can trade far from any model. The farm plan on each row is a starting point for research, not financial advice.

Token Radar is a research dashboard. Figures come from the DefiLlama protocol index snapshot of 14 November 2026; live-token protocols are set aside from the ranking and shown on their own tab, because their market capitalisation is a fact rather than a model. The board accounts for all 758 protocols in the snapshot: ranked pre-token, live-token, token-exists-but-mcap-unrecorded, and pre-token excluded by reason. Wrapped, staked and bridged products are kept off the board: their token already trades.
